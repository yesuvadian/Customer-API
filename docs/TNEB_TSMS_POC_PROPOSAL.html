The Problem

Four operational gaps that keep substation maintenance reactive, not proactive
#ChallengeConsequence for TNEB
1Fragmented recordsEquipment data scattered across registers, Excel sheets, and paper logs — no single source of truth for any substation
2Reactive maintenanceFailures discovered only when equipment breaks down, causing unplanned outages and expensive emergency repairs
3No test schedulingHealthiness tests missed or delayed — no automated reminders, no escalation chain, no compliance audit trail
4No performance insightVendor quality, repairer performance, and failure patterns invisible — every procurement decision made without data
Result: rising maintenance costs, unexpected transformer failures, and no data foundation for grid optimisation.
Equipment test history lives in paper registers — not searchable, not trended, not linked to the equipment record
Compliance calculated manually — no real-time overdue visibility per section, circle or zone
ALERT/CRITICAL classification relies on tester judgement, not configurable thresholds applied consistently

03

Benefits

Why this is the right platform for every testing role
  • One equipment register (UEIC) — every test, result and remedial action traces to a single tagged asset record
  • Role-aware dashboards for all 10 roles — AE/JE to CEE, each scoped to their span of responsibility
  • Automated test scheduling — configurable frequency templates with T+0 / T+7 / T+30 overdue escalation
  • Threshold-driven ALERT / CRITICAL — consistent classification across all testers, per equipment class
  • AI-assisted trend review — flags equipment drifting toward threshold before it crosses into ALERT, not after
  • AI failure predictions — failure probability, anomaly detection, and interval recommendations; officer approval required
  • AI-assisted comparison view — benchmark a unit's readings against sister equipment and across test types, surfacing outliers invisible in a single-record view
  • Maintenance-triggered procurement — 7 triggers routed automatically to the procurement bridge
  • SaaS on India-only data centres — 99.5% uptime SLA, ISO 27001, annual CERT-In VAPT; no on-premise hardware
04

POC Scope

In scope for the single-substation pilot
  • Agree with TNEB on the most critical test types to digitise first, for each equipment type
  • Digital execution of the agreed CT/PT/Power Transformer/LA CM/PM procedures
  • Historical data import — last 1 year of prior test records (PDF/Excel) loaded as backfilled equipment history
  • Live application execution — testers run day-to-day tests directly in TSMS, not on paper
  • Equipment-linked test history for every unit at the site
  • Auto-computed pass/fail & trend flags against standard thresholds
  • One governed report per test, generated on completion
  • Schedule visibility — due / overdue / completed, by equipment
  • Role-based workflow: tester → reviewer → close-out
05

Guidelines to Select the Site

What makes a strong first candidate substation
  • Full CT/PT/Power Transformer/LA fleet already in service — not a fresh commissioning
  • An existing routine testing programme, with a testing agency or crew already engaged
  • At least one full CM/PM cycle of history to digitise as a baseline reference
  • Site team able to share prior test records and the current testing schedule
  • Representative of TNEB's wider substation fleet, for replicability
06

Success Criteria

The pilot is successful when TNEB can show —
  • Agreed CT/PT/Power Transformer/LA test types executed digitally at the site
  • Last 1 year of prior test records imported and validated as each unit's equipment-history baseline
  • Testers running live, day-to-day tests directly in TSMS — not reverting to paper — by pilot close
  • Equipment-linked history live for 100% of in-scope units
  • Auto pass/fail flags matching engineers' manual judgement on trial tests
  • One-click report generation replacing manual drafting
  • Sign-off from the site testing team on day-to-day usability
